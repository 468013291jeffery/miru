# OFF LIMITS: THE HOCKEY BOYS

**MIRU M110 · US sports romance / investigative suspense · 12 chapters · 48 scenes**

All characters are fictional adults. The fictional Harbor Wolves, Red Harbor Falcons, facilities and league are not real-world NHL franchises.

## Part I. Player-facing home-page story

The morning after New York's Harbor Wolves lose a crucial game, you arrive for your first day as their media director—and walk straight into a press ambush.

Jace Walker, the team's golden-haired star scorer, moves toward you as the cameras flash. Captain Alexander Knight orders him to stop. Then someone spots a second camera: one that was planted before you arrived.

Alexander is a disciplined captain who seems to care more about his team than anyone else's feelings, yet he's terrified of losing another player. Jace thrives on the spotlight, but when his stunt crosses a line, he must learn that a sincere apology is not a publicity strategy. Goaltender Ethan Brooks takes care of everyone except himself. Across town, rival superstar Damon Steele holds evidence his own club would rather forget. And former captain Ryan Cole returns to the rink with a damaged knee, a ruined career, and a statement he signed three years ago.

They have different reasons to be near you. Some want to protect the team. Some want a scandal buried. Others are prepared to expose a dangerous cover-up, even if it costs them everything. None of them gets to decide how you feel, whom you trust, or whether you want romance at all.

That night, your brand-new employee badge unexpectedly unlocks an archive sealed for three years. An anonymous video starts playing. Seconds before the on-ice collision that ended Ryan's career, someone disabled an alarm.

Then the video starts deleting itself. Your first decision isn't whom to date. It's whom to trust with the only surviving copy—while someone on the other side of the network watches.

## Pre-story background · internal, not visible on home page

1. The two hockey clubs are fictional, based in an invented league environment near New York.
2. Three years earlier, a failure in the rink boards caused a collision and permanently affected Ryan's career. The initial report described it as an unexpected equipment fault.
3. The actual case involves expired fasteners reused by the contractor, and a decision to silence the alarm under commercial pressure. The guilty parties are established through independent evidence, not player inference.
4. Ryan signed an inaccurate statement under pressure. Alexander signed a confidentiality agreement. Their friendship fractured afterward.
5. Jace's earlier personal-boundary violation was captured by a camera planted for commercial exploitation. Jace remains responsible for his own conduct; the hidden-camera plot does not excuse it.
6. Ethan fears losing his position if his symptoms become public. Clinical judgment and independent review remain separate from romance or sports spectacle.
7. Damon holds access records from the visiting venue. He cooperates without asking for trade secrets or competitive favors.
8. The player is a professional media director and plays as themselves. Their feelings, past romantic history, and ultimate relationships are never pre-scripted.

### Endings and long-running companionship

The ending families are **reform and career continuity**, **independent sports investigation**, **explicitly chosen romance with one named adult**, **open-ended friendship**, and **an undecided new beginning**. Romance is never inferred from a trust score. A specific person's romance epilogue requires the player to affirmatively name them and express interest. There are five follow-up routes keyed `alexander`, `jace`, `ethan`, `damon`, `ryan`, each continuing through training, recovery, road trips, birthdays and seasonal events. There is no punishment for time away.
