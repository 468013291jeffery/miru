# CHAPTER 1｜The Exhibition With Your Name
⟦GOAL|Build a relationship through choices and consequences⟧
# SCENE｜An opening with your own key
Months after the waterfront hearing, the community center opens a photography exhibition curated by local residents. The first wall displays their names. Yours is one of them if you chose to be credited.
June: "People keep asking if the Morettis funded it. They didn't. We applied for a city grant and won."
You arrive on your own. There is no black car waiting and no romantic invitation hidden inside the program.
Dante appears only because the residents invited him as an ordinary visitor. He stands near the back, reading the captions carefully.
Dante: "Congratulations. I heard you did the hard part—getting the lighting to work."
If you prefer not to engage, he accepts a polite nod and leaves you to your evening.

# SCENE｜The photograph nobody owns
A journalist asks to photograph you and Dante together for a retrospective on the scandal.
Journalist: "People love a reunion. It makes a cleaner ending."
Elena intervenes only if you ask. The exhibition isn't anyone's reconciliation stage.
You can reject the photo, approve a group shot of residents, or allow a factual interview without romance questions.
June reminds the journalist that half of the work in the gallery comes from people displaced by the harbor project.
Dante: "Then put their pictures on the front page. They actually made something worth looking at."
For the first time you feel that your own future has enough momentum to move even when Dante is not in the room.

# SCENE｜Walking out under your own name
At closing, you lock the gallery door with a key issued to you by the community center. It's small and silver and yours.
Dante offers a quiet goodbye without a promise attached. You decide how to answer.
Your phone buzzes with messages from friends about the exhibit. You choose whose call to return first.
Nothing in the ending asks you to reconsider romance as proof of kindness. Independence is not a punishment or a temporary route waiting to be corrected.

# SCENE｜A witness, not a bridge
Nora agrees to testify about the records she copied with Luca. The hearing is held in a public courthouse with protections for her address and health information.
Nora: "I wasn't trying to become a symbol of loyalty. I wanted someone to stop stealing from tenants."
The judge asks the parties to keep private matters out of the case record. Elena nods with visible relief.
If you offer support, it is as a person who cares about due process, not as the former object of a billionaire's affection.
Dante attends in the public gallery. He does not approach Luca's representative or ask you to carry a message.

The lease on your new studio arrives by email. You read each page, sign it yourself, and take a photo of the bare rooms for a friend.
Your first visitor is June, carrying an overwatered plant from the community garden.
June: "I know it looks sad. Give it a week."
At the open-house celebration for the neighborhood, you see Dante across the courtyard. He is talking to Elena about the remaining restitution schedule, not watching for you.
He nods once, politely. There is no secret camera waiting to turn the moment into proof that you have reunited.

# SCENE｜Who gets the last word
A columnist asks you to comment on the rumor that your independence was a marketing maneuver arranged by the Moretti company.
You have documents that prove otherwise, but you do not owe strangers an account of your personal life. Elena can advise on the defamation issue if you choose.
Dante is told about the piece. He asks Elena not to speak for you and offers to submit only the public financial records relevant to the foundation.
Dante: "Her life belongs to her. That isn't a statement my lawyers need to improve."
You can publish a limited correction, ignore the story, or discuss it publicly at a community meeting.

# SCENE｜A future of your choosing
At sunset you sit on the steps of your studio, listening to the neighborhood settle. A student stops to ask when the next exhibition will open.
You tell her the date. She writes it on her hand.
The sound of a familiar car passes somewhere down the road and keeps going.
There may be friendship with Dante in the future, or not. Neither possibility is the moral of this story.
You lock your own front door and put the key in your pocket. The next chapter is yours.
