# CHAPTER 1｜Friday, Without an Audience
⟦GOAL|Build a relationship through choices and consequences⟧
# SCENE｜A burned birthday cake
Mara closes the restaurant early and hands Dante a paper box. The cake inside looks like it survived a small kitchen fire.
Mara: "He insisted on doing it himself. I told him to buy one. He said that defeated the point."
Dante: "For the record, the oven is a liar."
You are together now. No announcement appeared in the papers; no one acquired anyone else's surname. On the wall hangs a photo of the residents' new scholarship committee, not a portrait of Dante.
He reaches for your hand, remembers that you sometimes prefer to eat first, and grins when you catch him hesitating.
Dante: "I'm learning how to be someone's boyfriend without making it a corporate position. It turns out there are no board minutes."
The night is soft enough to forget the waterfront case—until Vince calls about a guard still logging your arrivals, despite the revised policy.

# SCENE｜The line in the logbook

# SCENE｜The vote that matters
June places the developer's proposal beside the resident-led plan. The numbers look similar; the difference is who controls the land after five years.
June: "If we give up the deed now, our children may have to rent their own garden from strangers."
Dante: "Then it's not a donation. It's an acquisition wearing nice shoes."
You can speak at the meeting, help residents compare the terms, or stay in the audience and let them lead. Whatever you choose, Dante does not use your relationship as an argument for the vote.
Outside afterward, he asks if you'd like to try a weekend trip together. Not a move. Not a lease. Just two days in a coastal town where nobody knows your last names.

# SCENE｜Returning with your own key
The weekend is full of small discoveries: he sleeps with the window open, you like breakfast on a schedule he has never mastered, and both of you need time alone after too much company.
Dante: "I thought a perfect home would solve everything. Instead I like that you can say 'I'm going home' and mean a place that's yours."
You return to the city with one suitcase each. The garden vote passes in the residents' favor after an independent title review.
Dante holds your hand outside your front door, then waits while you open it yourself. Living together remains a possibility you may revisit, not a reward the story forces on you.

# SCENE｜The gala and the postcard
Luca's postcard remains on the table. Dante doesn't try to trace the postmark.
Dante: "I want to get on a plane and find him. I won't. I can wait for the invitation he has the right to make."
At the gala, June accepts an award for the residents' council. The emcee begins to introduce Dante as the man who saved the harbor. He gently corrects him.
Dante: "The council did that work. Please say their names."
A reporter asks you when the wedding will be. You can answer, redirect, or decline. Dante keeps silent until you ask him to speak.
After the event, he takes off his jacket, hands you a paper cup of tea, and asks what kind of future you want to keep choosing.

# SCENE｜What commitment looks like
No wedding appears as a surprise twist. No proposal arrives because the music swells.
Dante: "I can offer you loyalty, the truth even when it makes me look bad, and the freedom to decide again tomorrow. If someday marriage means something to us, we can discuss it then."
You decide what to do with the corrected invitation. The name printed on it is yours, exactly as you chose it.
He walks beside you into a night that feels less like an ending than the first ordinary chapter of two adult lives.
